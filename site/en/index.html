Today's to-dos,
on one sheet of paper.

A notepad for writing down to-dos and ticking them off. The top shows what's on now and how long is left, and a paper cat brings your reminders. There's no account; everything is saved as files on your computer.

Tour

One screen at a time

01 · The notepad

A notepad that puts now first

One sheet of paper with Today and Ahead tabs. The day ribbon at the top writes what's on now in large handwriting, with the time left in a ring beside it. What's next and today's list follow below.

02 · Timetable

Your day, unfolded

Click the day ribbon and the day unfolds into a timetable right there. Drag a block and it follows the mouse, then settles on a quarter hour. Drag its top or bottom edge to change its length.

03 · Focus

Focus on one thing

Type ‘report 25 min focus’ and the notepad turns into a single card. When time is up, pick Done, 5 more min or Not now. It can run Pomodoro rounds too.

04 · Alarms

Alarms on a paper slip

Pick the days, the sound and the snooze. When it's time, a slip pops up with the cat shaking a bell. For the first 1.5 seconds it ignores the keyboard, so you can't dismiss it by accident while typing somewhere else.

05 · Ahead

Drag to reschedule

This week and next, day by day, with events and due dates in one place. Drag a to-do onto another day and its due date moves with it.

06 · Desk

Calendar and settings on the Desk

Calendar, history, settings and help live in the Desk window. Keep the notepad small on screen, and open the Desk when you need to look further ahead. The calendar also shows lunar dates and holiday names.

A cat for company

It breathes and wanders above the Dock (the taskbar on Windows), and walks over with a to-do when its reminder is due. Finish one and it celebrates. Now and then it asks ‘A 5-minute break?’; answer with a button and a 5-minute timer starts.

Windows

Mac and Windows, one list

Choose the same shared folder as the storage location on both computers and they share one list. Edits made at almost the same time are all kept, and a note changed on both is merged. Your cloud service moves the files.

Night paper

When the system is in dark mode, every window turns the color of paper in a room with the lights off. You can also fix it to day or night under Desk → Appearance → Day & night. Text contrast meets the web accessibility standard (WCAG AA) by day and by night.

Tap-tap cat

Type, and a cat taps along in the corner

The tap-tap cat sits on a cushion in a corner of the screen and taps with its paws while you type. You set it up in Desk → Cat, apart from the desktop cat.

  • Where it taps alongUnder Tap along, pick Off, In this app or In all apps. With In this app it comes out while you type and slips away 3 seconds after you stop.
  • Any of four cornersPick a corner of the screen under Corner. Clicks go through to the window behind, so it never gets in the way, and it shows over full-screen apps too.
  • Cats in four sizesThe tap-tap cat and the desktop cat can each be Small, Regular, Large or Extra large, apart from each other. When the desktop cat grows, its speech bubble and question buttons move with it.
  • It never knows which keyIt only knows that a key was pressed, and never reads or keeps what you typed. ‘In all apps’ needs Input Monitoring permission on the Mac, and hasn’t been tried on a real Windows PC yet. The privacy page has the details.

Calendar widgets

A paper calendar on your desktop

Pick from seven widgets, from Today to a wall calendar for the month, and keep up to four. Lunar dates and Korean public holidays show from the start. Add them in Desk → Events → Desktop widgets.

  • Click a day for its slipClick a day on a widget to see everything on it. Type there as you would in the notepad’s input line, and it goes straight in.
  • Four sizesEach widget can be Small, Regular, Large or Extra large. Text, cells and margins all scale together, so a large widget doesn’t leave cells empty or cut names short. Sizes that don’t fit your screen are dimmed.
  • Readable when fadedPick an opacity from 20 to 100%. Each day sits on a paper tile, so the text stays readable when the widget is faint. How it looks on your own wallpaper hasn’t been checked on a real screen yet.
  • Drag it by the earsDrag the cat’s ears at the top right to move a widget, or click them for a menu with Size and Keep on top. Widgets sit on the desktop, below other windows, at first.

Repeats on the calendar

Repeats show on the days ahead

A repeating to-do like ‘Recycling every Monday’ shows on every day it comes back. The Desk calendar, the widgets, the day slip and the wall calendar all show it.

  • Only the next one is boldThe next time you’ll do it is bold, and the ones after it are faint. Click a faint line and the notepad shows that to-do.
  • Daily repeats fold into ⟲Daily and weekday repeats show as ⟲ and a count next to the date instead of their names, so events and deadlines get the lines first. Hover over ⟲ to see the names.
  • Open events from the keyboardPick a day on the Desk calendar and press Tab to go to its first event. Move with ↑ ↓ and press Return (Enter on Windows) to open its edit slip. Esc takes you back to the day.
  • Lunar dates stay wholeWhen the date line runs short of room, the lunar date shortens to ‘10.1’, and the repeat count goes first if it’s still too tight. Nothing spills out of the cell.

Fifteen papers

Pick a paper, and every window follows

Choose one in Desk → Appearance → Paper, and the notepad, calendar, widgets, slips, Desk and notes drawer all change to it. There are eleven light papers and four dark ones.

  • Seven new papersSticky note, Manuscript paper, Blue lined notebook, Lavender, Recycled paper, Night sky and Chalkboard. Whether Sticky note and Chalkboard are easy on the eyes over time hasn’t been checked yet.
  • At nightA light paper changes at night to the one you pick under At night: Night paper, Ink, Night sky, Chalkboard, or no change.
  • A paper of its own for the drawerThe notes drawer starts on the notepad’s paper. Turn off Same as the notepad in Desk → Notes drawer to pick one just for the drawer.
  • Nothing changes when you updateYou start with Day paper, and Night paper at night. A paper you already picked for the notes drawer stays.

To-do spaces

Sorted, still one sheet

Sort your to-dos into spaces such as Work, Home and Study. Today still shows them all on one sheet, with only the space’s color band at the left of each row.

  • Show one spacePick it from ‘All spaces ▾’ in the header, or press ⌥⌘1–9 (Ctrl+Shift+1–9 on Windows). Everything shows again at 4 a.m. the next day.
  • # as you typeWrite ‘send the quote by Friday #work’ and it goes into Work. The start of the name, like ‘#wo’, is enough.
  • Move and manageRight-click a to-do to move it to another space. Set names, colors and order under Desk → Spaces.
  • Nothing changes until you make oneWith no spaces, the notepad looks just as before. While you look at one space, reminders from the others still come.

Group by space

Work, home and study at a glance

Gather today’s to-dos by space. Turn it on from ‘All spaces ▾’ in the header, or press ⌥⌘G (Ctrl+Shift+G on Windows).

  • Cards or bookmarksEach space gathers on a small card, or in a group with a bookmark at its head. Pick the style under Desk → Spaces.
  • Columns when it’s wideWhile grouped, the notepad can be up to 1,200px wide. With room to spare, each space gets its own column, and ‘+ Add to Work’ at the bottom writes straight into it.
  • Drag to moveDrag a to-do onto another group to change its space, or use ⌥⌘↑ ⌥⌘↓ (Ctrl+Alt+↑ Ctrl+Alt+↓ on Windows). Pick several to move them at once.
  • Repeating to-dos tooWrite ‘Drink water every day #home’ and each day’s to-do goes into that space. To-dos in no space gather under General.

Automatic backups

Backed up once a day, by itself

The storage folder is saved as a backup file (.jongi) once a day, and once more before anything overwrites your list, such as an import or a restore.

  • Storage and backups in one placeDesk → Storage & backup starts with ‘Where should your to-dos live?’ and tells you in one line when the last backup was. The backup list, export and import are under Show details.
  • Pick one to restoreChoose a backup, then Merge or Replace. Merge keeps what’s here and brings back only what was deleted since. Either way, what’s here now is backed up first.
  • Only on this computerBackups go to this computer’s backups folder, not the storage folder, so they don’t travel to other devices even with a shared folder. Up to 14 daily backups are kept.
  • When you move the folderPicking another storage folder tells you first that your current list moves there. Overwriting backs up the list from before.

Notes drawer

Notes live in a drawer at the screen’s edge

Pull the drawer out from the edge of the screen in any app to read a note, jot one down, or edit it in place. Notes from the notepad’s old Notes tab are in the drawer’s Notes folder.

  • Out from any appPress ⇧⌘D (Ctrl+Shift+D on Windows) or click the cat-paw handle at the edge. Clicking another app doesn’t close it.
  • Folders are bookmarksEach bookmark beside the drawer is a folder. Notes are grouped into Pinned, Today, Yesterday, Last week and then by month, and a link or phone number shows as a small label on the card.
  • Right-click to sortA note, a bookmark and empty drawer space each have a menu. Move to a folder, duplicate and sort from there, or give a single note its own paper.
  • Paper and backdropThe fifteen papers are the same as the notepad’s. The backdrop is Solid by default, with Frosted, Clear and None to choose from. Frosted on Windows hasn’t been seen on a real PC yet.

Notes drawer

A wider drawer, easier to search

1.46 (Windows 1.31) polishes the notes drawer. In the Larger view it widens toward the screen, and the bottom line shows the character count.

  • Search by folderFound notes are grouped by folder. Filter them with the Unticked, Links and Pinned chips, or search only the folder you’re in.
  • Titles onlyIn the folder name ▾ menu (⌘L, Ctrl+L on Windows), choose Titles only to show each note on one line. Pick the sort order there too; each folder remembers its view.
  • Deleted notes for 30 daysDeleted notes stay for 30 days. Deleted notes in the folder name ▾ menu puts them back in their folder.
  • Checkboxes and the link boxCheckboxes show while you edit a note, and they’re bigger to click. Select text and press ⌘K (Ctrl+K on Windows) to add a link in a small box under the note.

Note font

Choose the note font and spacing

Under Note font in Desk → Notes drawer, pick the font, code font, text size, line spacing and paragraph spacing. They apply to every note in the drawer and on its cards.

  • Same as the notepadThe notepad and the notes drawer lay out their font settings the same way, so once you know one, you know the other.
  • Code fontD2Coding can be the code font. A Korean letter is two Latin letters wide in it, so code lines keep their columns.
  • Looks the way you writeBold, headings, lists, checkboxes and code look that way as you type. Change them with the format (Aa) button on an open note or keys such as ⌘B.
  • Checklist chipOn a note card, a bar inside the ‘2/4’ chip shows how much of the checklist is done.

Also new

What else changed

Mac 1.49 and Windows 1.34 also touch the notepad, the widgets, speed and the Claude connection.

  • Keep the notepad on the desktopThe pin at the top of the notepad offers Regular, Keep on top and On the desktop. On the desktop, the notepad stays behind other windows like a widget, and comes forward only for a moment when you open it with its shortcut.
  • Quiet while you move the mouseOn the Mac, moving the mouse in other apps hardly wakes Jongi-nyang anymore. While the pointer is far from the cats and widgets, it checks where it is only a few times a second.
  • Windows widgets stay on the desktopA widget with Keep on top turned off no longer jumps over other windows when you click it. It stays on the desktop, as on the Mac.
  • A new Claude Code commandOn Windows, the Claude Code connection command now runs through Jongi-nyang itself, and other programs can no longer use Jongi-nyang as if it were Node. Commands you copied before still work.

How it's built

Built twice, kept identical

Swift (SwiftUI + AppKit) on the Mac, Electron + TypeScript on Windows, built separately. They share no code. Both have to pass the same tables of test cases, which keeps their behavior the same.

136 rowsof input-line cases both apps pass character for character
582 + 420Windows tests, plus about 420 Mac checks (Windows 1.30, Mac 1.45)
61% → 0.7%Mac idle CPU with the notepad open
WCAG AAfor the app's text colors, day and night (except deliberately faint hints). Anything below it fails the check
  • Case tables come firstHow ‘meeting tomorrow 3pm’ is read, down to the words on the chip, is one row. The Swift and TypeScript tests must both produce that exact string.
  • Screens are checked by rendering themOn the Mac, about 2,100 real screens are captured across 3 languages, day and night. On Windows, every window is redrawn at different sizes, scale factors and fonts to catch clipped text.
  • Where the 61% CPU came fromThe blinking ‘now’ line in the day ribbon was redrawing the whole notepad every frame. Drawing that line on its own brought idle CPU down to 0.7%. In the same release, typing latency on the Mac fell from 0.46 s to 0.01 s and launch from 2.7 s to 1.0 s. On Windows, version 1.24 cut memory after 10 minutes from 692 MB to 457 MB (measured in Electron on macOS).
  • Not done yetThe Windows version has only run in Electron on macOS, never on a real Windows PC. Neither app is code-signed.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is it really free?

Yes. No payments and no locked features. The source code is on GitHub under the Apache License 2.0.

Do I need an account? Where are my notes kept?

No account and no server. Everything is saved as files in Jongi-nyang’s folder on your computer, or in a folder you choose. The privacy page has the details. Change it under Desk → Storage & backup.

Can I use it on a Mac and a Windows PC together?

Yes. Choose the same shared folder (iCloud Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox and so on) as the storage location on both. Each device can also decide which spaces it shows.

Does it connect to Google Calendar?

No. I built it over five releases and it worked, but I took it out. I didn't want work plans mixing into my personal space, and the people testing it said the same. The full reasoning is in the decision log (Korean).

Why is it blocked the first time I open it?

The builds aren't code-signed. On the Mac, right-click the app in Applications and choose Open; if it’s still blocked, click Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security. On Windows, choose More info → Run anyway in SmartScreen.

Which languages and systems?

Korean, English and Dutch. It runs on macOS 14 or later on Apple Silicon, and on Windows 10/11 x64. The Windows version has only been checked in Electron on macOS, not yet on a real Windows PC.

Check what you downloaded

Each release lists the SHA-256 value of every file, in the checksums file (SHA256SUMS) and in the release notes. If your file gives the same value, it’s the one we uploaded. If it differs, don’t open it; delete it.

  • Mac Terminal: shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/JongiNyang-1.49.0.dmg
  • Windows PowerShell: Get-FileHash "$HOME\Downloads\JongiNyang-1.34.0-x64.exe" -Algorithm SHA256
