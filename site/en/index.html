A note for writing down to-dos and ticking them off. The top shows what's on now and how long is left, and a paper cat brings your reminders. There's no account; everything is saved as files on your computer.

Tour

One screen at a time

01 · The note

A note that puts now first

One sheet of paper with Today, Ahead and Memo tabs. The day strip at the top writes what's on now in large handwriting, with the time left in a ring beside it. What's next and today's list follow below.

02 · Timetable

Your day, unfolded

Click the day strip and the day unfolds into a timetable right there. Drag a block and it follows the mouse, then settles on a quarter hour. Drag its top or bottom edge to change its length.

03 · Focus

Focus on one thing

Type “report 25 min focus” and the note turns into a single card. When time is up, pick Done, 5 more min or Not now. It can run Pomodoro rounds too.

04 · Alarms

Alarms on a paper slip

Pick the days, the sound and the snooze. When it's time, a slip pops up with the cat shaking a bell. For the first 1.5 seconds it ignores the keyboard, so you can't dismiss it by accident while typing somewhere else.

05 · Ahead

Drag to reschedule

This week and next, day by day, with events and due dates in one place. Drag a to-do onto another day and its due date moves with it.

06 · Desk

Calendar and settings on the Desk

Calendar, history, settings and help live in the Desk window. Keep the note small on screen, and open the Desk when you need to look further ahead.

Mac and Windows, one list

Choose the same cloud folder as the storage location on both computers and they share one list. Edits made at nearly the same time both survive, and a memo edited on both is merged line by line and character by character.

Night paper

Put the system in dark mode and every window switches to night paper: the color of paper in a room with the lights off. Day or night, all text passes WCAG AA contrast.

How it's built

Built twice, kept identical

Swift (SwiftUI + AppKit) on the Mac, Electron + TypeScript on Windows, built separately. They share no code. Both have to pass the same tables of test cases, which keeps their behavior the same.

136 rowsof input-line cases both apps pass character for character
471 + 300Windows tests, plus about 300 Mac checks
61% → 0.7%Mac idle CPU with the note open
WCAG AAfor all text, day and night. Anything below it fails the check
  • Case tables come firstHow “meeting tomorrow 3pm” is read, down to the words on the chip, is one row. The Swift and TypeScript tests must both produce that exact string.
  • Screens are checked by rendering themOn the Mac, about 1,500 real screens are captured across 3 languages, day and night. On Windows, every window is redrawn at different sizes, scale factors and fonts to catch clipped text.
  • Where the 61% CPU came fromThe blinking “now” line in the day strip was redrawing the whole note every frame. Drawing that line on its own brought idle CPU down to 0.7%. In the same release, typing latency on the Mac fell from 0.46 s to 0.01 s and launch from 2.7 s to 1.0 s. On Windows, memory after 10 minutes went from 692 MB to 457 MB.
  • Not done yetThe Windows version has only run in Electron on macOS, never on a real Windows PC. Neither app is code-signed.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is it really free?

Yes. No payments and no locked features. The source code is on GitHub under the Apache License 2.0.

Do I need an account? Where are my notes kept?

No account and no server. Everything is saved as files in a folder you choose. Change it under Desk → Storage & backup.

Can I use it on a Mac and a Windows PC together?

Yes. Choose a folder both computers can see (iCloud Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox and so on) as the storage location.

Does it connect to Google Calendar?

No. I built it over five releases and it worked, but I took it out. I didn't want work plans mixing into my personal space, and the people testing it said the same. The full reasoning is in the decision log (Korean).

Why is it blocked the first time I open it?

The builds aren't code-signed. On the Mac, right-click the app in Applications and choose Open. On Windows, choose More info → Run anyway in SmartScreen.

Which languages and systems?

Korean, English and Dutch. It runs on macOS 14 or later on Apple Silicon, and on Windows 10/11 x64. The Windows version has only been checked in Electron on macOS, not yet on a real Windows PC.
